CIM Model Exchange in a Global Grid

Embracing Variation Without Losing Meaning

Executive Summary

As power systems become increasingly interconnected, the IEC Common Information Model (CIM) has evolved into the global semantic foundation for exchanging grid models across utilities, vendors, and regions.

A defining feature of CIM is its deliberate allowance for multiple valid representations of the same physical assets—reflecting different operational, planning, and regulatory contexts. This article explains how identity‑centric governance, profile‑based exchange, and transparency enable abstraction without semantic or operational loss.

1. Identity‑Centric Modeling

CIM anchors interoperability on persistent identity (mRID), not identical structural representation. This allows different internal abstractions of the same physical asset to coexist safely.

2. Topology Abstraction

Internal models frequently retain node‑breaker detail, while exchange models collapse these structures into bus‑branch equivalents where switching detail is unnecessary.

3. Boundary Exchange vs Internal Detail

CIM profiles define the scope of exchange. Internal models remain autonomous and richer, while boundary models focus on identity and connectivity.

4. Profile‑Based Exchange Pipeline

Interoperability is realized through contextual profiles and constraints derived from the CIM base model.

5. Open Questions for Industry Consultation

  • How much abstraction is acceptable before operational meaning is lost?
  • Should identity (mRID) always be preserved across abstraction levels?
  • Do we need globally standardized reduced‑order CIM profiles?
  • Should modeling assumptions be machine‑readable?
  • Who bears responsibility for semantic misinterpretation?
