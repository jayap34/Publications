CIM Model Exchange in a Global Grid

Embracing Variation Without Losing Meaning

Executive Summary

As power systems become increasingly interconnected, the IEC Common Information Model (CIM) has emerged as the global semantic foundation for exchanging network models. Crucially, CIM permits multiple valid representations of the same physical assets, reflecting different operational, planning, and regulatory contexts.

This article explains how identity‑centric governance, profile‑based exchange, and transparency allow utilities to embrace abstraction without losing semantic integrity. It concludes with open questions for global industry consultation.

1. Identity‑Centric Modeling

CIM anchors interoperability on persistent identity (mRID), not identical structure. This allows different abstractions of the same physical asset to coexist safely.

2. Topology Abstraction

Utilities frequently maintain node‑breaker models internally while exchanging bus‑branch equivalents for coordination and studies.

3. Boundary Exchange vs Internal Detail

CIM profiles define what is exchanged. Internal models may retain richer detail, while boundary models focus on identity and connectivity.

4. Profile‑Based Exchange Pipeline

CIM interoperability is realized through contextual profiles and constraints derived from the base model.

5. Open Questions for Industry Consultation

  • How much abstraction is acceptable before operational meaning is lost?
  • Should identity (mRID) always be preserved across abstraction levels?
  • Do we need standardized reduced‑order CIM profiles?
  • Should modeling assumptions be machine‑readable?
  • Who bears responsibility for semantic misinterpretation?
